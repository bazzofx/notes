
## Traditional Anti-Malware Detection
This approach requires security vendors to continuously collect malware samples, create matching signature files for those samples, and distribute those signature files as updates for their endpoint security products to all of their customers. ## Container-Based Approach
![](https://cdn5.dcbstatic.com/files/p/a/paloaltonetworks_docebosaas_com/1789056000/TLlXcBM9sFR53h65Xizj0A/scorm/9a6d4330ad2417ef5163611f16f77034b2d7de9345e84df969d83eb88afa921b/course/en/assets/5ed1965cd2816b17416d58be/original.png)
Container-based endpoint protection wraps a protective virtual barrier around vulnerable processes while they’re running. If a process is malicious, the container detects it and shuts it down, preventing it from damaging other legitimate processes or files on the endpoint.

### Drawbacks of the Container-Based Approach
Container-based endpoint protection demands significant computing resources, has been circumvented by attacks, and requires knowledge of protected applications. It cannot protect most proprietary or industry-specific software, and even browser plugins may malfunction within containers.


## Application Allowlisting
![](https://cdn5.dcbstatic.com/files/p/a/paloaltonetworks_docebosaas_com/1789056000/TLlXcBM9sFR53h65Xizj0A/scorm/9a6d4330ad2417ef5163611f16f77034b2d7de9345e84df969d83eb88afa921b/course/en/assets/5ec58eb3ec7ca7426f9e570a/original.png)
Application allowlisting is another endpoint protection technique that is commonly used to prevent end users from running unauthorized applications, such as malware, on their endpoints.

### **Application Allowlisting Drawbacks**
Cloud, mobile, consumerization, and BYOD/BYOA trends make application allowlisting hard to enforce. Allowlisted apps can still be exploited, giving attackers full endpoint control. They then run malicious code entirely in memory—creating no new files or executables—rendering allowlisting ineffective.

## Anomaly-Based Detection
Endpoint security approaches that use mathematical algorithms to detect unusual activity on endpoints are known as heuristics-based, behavior-based, or anomaly detection solutions.
This approach relies on first establishing an accurate baseline of what is considered “normal” activity. This approach has been around for many years and requires a very large dataset to reduce the number of false-positives.


## Host-Based Intrusion Prevention Systems
Host-based intrusion prevention systems (HIPS) are another approach to endpoint protection.

A HIPS relies on an agent installed on the endpoint to detect malware. Since a HIPS can be either signature-based or anomaly-based, it is susceptible to the same issues as other signature-based and anomaly-based endpoint protection approaches. HIPS software often causes significant performance degradation on endpoints.
