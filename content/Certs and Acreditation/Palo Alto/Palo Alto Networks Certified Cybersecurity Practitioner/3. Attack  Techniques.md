## Business Email Compromise (BEC)
Business email compromise (BEC) is one of the most prevalent types of cyberattacks that organizations face today

### Types of Phishing Attacks

**Spear Phishing:** Targeted phishing using personal details to appear credible, often via email or links, increasing success rates.

**Whaling:** Spear phishing aimed at senior executives, often disguised as legal subpoenas or serious complaints.

**Watering Hole:** Compromising websites frequented by targets, infecting visitors with malware via drive-by downloads.

**Pharming:** Redirecting legitimate website traffic to fake sites by modifying hosts files or poisoning DNS.

## Bots and Botnets
Bots and botnets are notoriously difficult for organizations to detect and defend against using traditional anti-malware solutions.
Botnets are created by cybercriminals to harvest computing resources (bots). Control of botnets (through C2 servers) can then be sold or rented out to other cybercriminals.

### Challenges to Disabling a Botnet
The key to “taking down” or “decapitating” a botnet is to separate the bots (infected endpoints) from their brains (C2 servers). If the bots cannot get to their servers, they cannot get new instructions, upload stolen data, or do anything that makes botnets so unique and dangerous. Although this approach may seem straightforward, disabling a botnet presents many challenges.

### Actions for Disabling a Botnet
**Disabling Internet Access:** Remove infected devices from network first, severing C2 connections and preventing infection spread.

**Monitor Local Network Activity:** Apply patches and updates; image and cleanse endpoints persistently connecting to C2 or attack targets.

**Remove Infected Devices and Botnet Software:** Ongoing process; dormant infections may reactivate later via C2 signals.

**Install Current Patches:** ISPs follow Shared Responsibility Model, but fully disabling C2 clusters remains extremely difficult.

### Distributed Denial-of-Service Attack
A DDoS attack is a type of cyberattack in which extremely high volumes of network traffic such as packets, data, or transactions are sent to the target victim’s network to make their network and systems (such as an e-commerce website or other web application) unavailable or unusable.

### Financial Botnets

## Advanced Persistent Threats
**Advanced:** Attackers use sophisticated malware and exploits, with skills and resources to develop new attack tools and techniques.

**Persistent:** APTs can span years; attackers pursue specific objectives slowly and methodically to avoid detection.

**Threat:** APTs are deliberate and focused, not opportunistic, and are designed to cause real damage.

## Wi-Fi Challenges
**Public Airwaves:** Wi-Fi uses shared public airwaves (2.4GHz/5GHz), also used by Bluetooth, making it vulnerable to congestion and collisions.

**Wi-Fi Network:** Wi-Fi settings are openly published and broadcast; disable SSID broadcast to make networks harder for attackers to discover.

**Mobile Device & Customer Apps:** Mobile devices are vulnerable; BYOD complicates management, users neglect patching, install risky apps, and disable security for performance.
![[Pasted image 20260910113929.png]]

## Evil Twin
Attackers set up fake wireless access points bridging to real networks, baiting victims with "free Wi-Fi." They mimic legitimate access point names, but can't target specific victims since the attack relies on victims connecting voluntarily.

## Jasager
Devices automatically reconnect to previously visited wireless networks, remembering their details without requiring you to choose an access point—whether at home, the office, or a coffee shop


## SSLstrip
After connecting to a compromised or fake Wi-Fi network, attackers intercept traffic, redirect browsers to their own servers, and control all content the victim sees.

![[Pasted image 20260910114442.png]]

### **SSLstrip Strategy**
SSLstrip removes SSL encryption from secure sessions on compromised Wi-Fi, intercepting SSL requests and displaying a fake padlock favicon to deceive users into believing the connection is secure.
