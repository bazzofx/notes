# Professional Artist Sound Extraction Prompt Template

# Restrictions
- Maximum 1000 characters final output prompt

## Recording Era

Describe the technological and aesthetic period of the recording.

- Specify decade and recording philosophy
- Mention tape vs digital character
- Define loudness style and dynamic range
- Describe tonal color and stereo philosophy

**Example language:**

> Late-90s analog-to-digital transitional recording aesthetic with moderate tape saturation, wide stereo imaging, preserved transient dynamics, and warm low-mid harmonic density. Slightly imperfect human timing with organic room interaction rather than grid-locked quantization.

---

## The Instrumentation Mechanics

Describe how instruments behave, not just what instruments exist.

- Rhythm section movement
- Harmonic density
- Layering philosophy
- Groove mechanics
- Humanization
- Arrangement pacing
- Texture interaction

**Example language:**

> Tight syncopated drum programming layered with live percussive accents. Rounded electric bass occupying the 80–140Hz region with legato phrasing. Sparse harmonic instrumentation leaving intentional negative space between phrases. Guitar textures processed as atmospheric layers rather than lead elements. Melodic motifs repeated hypnotically with gradual automation-based evolution.

---

## The Vocal Frequency & Delivery

Describe the psychoacoustic behavior of the vocal.

- Vocal register
- Compression behavior
- Presence region
- Sibilance handling
- Emotional delivery
- Cadence mechanics
- Intimacy vs projection
- Tonal saturation

**Example language:**

> Intimate close-mic vocal delivery with dominant presence around 2–5kHz and controlled air band extension above 10kHz. Breath texture retained intentionally for emotional proximity. Moderate serial compression with fast attack control and smooth optical leveling. Slight saturation emphasizing upper harmonic grit while preserving softness in consonants. Melodic phrasing alternates between conversational rhythm and elongated sustained notes.

---

## The Specific Studio Effects

Describe the exact spatial and tonal processing chain.

- Reverb architecture
- Delay timing
- Modulation style
- Saturation profile
- Stereo treatment
- Parallel processing
- Automation behavior
- Master bus texture

**Example language:**

> Short plate reverbs combined with tempo-synced quarter-note delays tucked low in the mix. Subtle chorus modulation widening background elements without affecting mono compatibility. Tape-style saturation on buses adding harmonic cohesion. Parallel drum compression enhancing punch while preserving transient snap. Master chain retains moderate glue compression with soft high-frequency roll-off and restrained limiting for dynamic musicality.
